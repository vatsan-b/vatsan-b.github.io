Tip-transforming Guide Catheters based on Auxetic Metamaterials

Coronary intervention and endovascular therapies require threading a catheter through blood vessels to reopen the artery. In complex PCI, forces required to push devices past a blockage can drive the catheter out of its position, which can damage the artery wall or cause bleeding. To solve this, I help build catheters with transforming tips made of auxetic metamaterials. They have the ability to expand, bend, or extend, and can anchor inside arteries and provide stable support when needed during surgeries. I am building tools and studying how the kinematics and geometry of the HSA (that forms the tip) affect its mechanical behavior, especially for millimeter-scale medical devices. This will let us pick designs that are safe and effective for peripheral, endo- and neurovascular therapies. In the future, this system can potentially enable autonomous anchoring.

Flexible Sensor-Integrated End Effector for Underwater Manipulation

Underwater manipulation is a challenging task due to low visibility and strong currents at depth, and mere visual feedback is insufficient to assess grasp stability. Tactile sensing can complement vision and support human-supervised subsea manipulation tasks. To demonstrate the use of flexible barometric tactile sensors on curved geometries, I developed an end-effector prototype which allowed building a training platform for gathering a large amount of data for tactile sensing. I co-developed a data collection procedure to characterize slip in the system that mimics the condition occuring during underwater grasping. This can also be used to train slip detection classifiers which will allow robots to detect slip. Additionally, I developed tools that provided feedback on finger position, in addition to providing tactile information.

Handed Shearing Auxetics (HSA) and High Grip Strength Soft Grippers

Soft robots are ideal for delicate manipulation and safe human-robot interaction, but their lack of rigid structures limits their use in real-world tasks. I integrated a torsionally-rigid strain limiting layer (TR-SLL) into a handed shearing auxetics (HSA)-based soft gripper. I conducted a finite element parametric design study to determine the optimal TR-SLL layout that minimized out-of-plane deformation without affecting the gripper’s ability to bend in-plane. The soft HSA gripper was able to lift a payload of over 1 kg in a pinch grasp, and 5 kg when the load goes through the TR-SLL. It reached a peak pinch force of 5.8 N, and a peak planar caging force of 14.5 N, which is substantial for lifting objects perpendicular to gravity. The gripper achieved an 86% success rate when we tested with 43 YCB objects.

Sensing-Capable Parallel Jaw Gripper for Learning Soft Fruit Harvesting

Fresh blackberries are still picked by hand, and growers struggle to find enough workers every season. Robots could help, but harvesting soft fruits like blackberries requires careful force control. Too much force damages the fruit, too little causes it to slip. To teach robots, I built a sensorized parallel-jaw gripper integrated with tactile sensors that works with a blackberry proxy that is designed to simulate real fruit properties and variations. This gripper allows human-controlled data collection to train robots that can judge berry ripeness and apply the right grasp pressure. Most parallel-jaw grippers rely on two independent motors and linear rails, while this design uses a mirrored Scott-Russell linkage driven by a single linear actuator. This mechanism could be used to build an automated, affordable training platform, though this remains an open direction for future work.

Electrically-Driven Soft Arms based on Mechanical Metamaterials

Soft robot arms usually require a hanging configuration to lift significant payloads. Expanding the work on HSA grippers, we designed and built an electrically-driven soft arm with auxetic metamaterials, and flex shafts that bend and extend while transmitting torque through the system. This arm can be mounted horizontally or vertically, and can perform tasks such as pipe inspection and underwater docking with a high degree of compliance. The HSA arm lifted a 603 g mustard bottle while supporting its own weight, and up to 2.3 kg vertically.

Enhanced Pneumatic Network Actuators (Pneu-Net)

Pneu-Nets are the most common form of soft gripper, but they are made usually of silicone with no skeleton to resist twisting. So objects that are being picked slip out of the grasp under heavier loads. We introduced the 3D-printed torsion-resistant strain limiting layers to this system, which reduced out-of-plane deformations by up to 97% compared to a benchmark Pneu-Net actuator from the Soft Robotics Toolkit. We later carried this over to the HSA gripper above.
